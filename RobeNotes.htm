This type of robe (ποδηρης ≈ 'podērēs' ≈ a long robe or tunic that reaches down to the feet) along with the high girdle was also worn by princes & kings in Israel and other ANE nations.
In the LXX, this included the robes of Jonathan (1 Sam 18:4), Saul (1 Sam 24:4) & the 'princes of the sea' (Ezek 26:16), who were probably merchant-princes of Tyre.
To Greco-Roman eyes, Jesus' attire may well have appeared like one of the 'kings of the east' (cf. 16:12f.). The dreaded Parthian kings across the Euphrates, who had long resisted and rivalled Roman power, wore decorated long robes and tunics, and their gods were often described as 'high-girt' (eg. Yasht 15:54-57).
Hence, the risen Christ, once a lowly peasant crucified as a naked criminal, is now dressed like a king!
- Barclay's commentary
