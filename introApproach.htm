What approach does this guide take to interpreting Revelation?
Information about context … ?
“And though St. John saw many strange monsters in his vision, he saw no creature so wild as one of his own commentators.” — Orthodoxy by G.K. Chesteron (1908)
Over 1900+ years of Christian history, experience & scholarship, a range of approaches to interpretation of biblical texts have developed, including for John's Apocalypse. A brief search on a web browser will soon identify 4 main approaches taken by commentators when interpreting Revelation.
The approach taken in this guide is built upon the following principles:
  • The primary meaning of any text is the meaning it held for the original author & audience.

Overall the approach taken in this guide is a 'literary' reading of the text. This may be understood as 'grammatico-historical exegesis,' without limiting that to a 'literalist' reading of the text. 'Literalist' readings always fail to fully take into account the range of strategies used by the extremely creative writer of this often-breath-taking text. Such readings also tend to impose upon the words of the texts meanings which clearly come not from the context of the original author or audience, but from the context of modern readers; this would be 'eisogesis' rather than 'exegesis'!
