The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Apocalypse
                             Introduction
                             Apocalypse ... what Apocalypse?!
Launch in!
The word 'apocalypse' has meanings in conversational language & Western popular culture that are actually misleading for those seeking to read & understand the New Testament 'Apocalypse', the book of Revelation.

Due to the global influence of both Western literature & other media over the last 2 centuries, the word 'apocalypse' conjures up primariy images of 'the end of the world as we know it' global (or at least national) catastrophes in most people's minds. These books, radio broadcasts, TV shows and movies (and even popular songs) tell stories that portray people in situations where they are:
  • trying to prevent the catastrophe, or
  • trying to survive the catastrophe, or
  • trying to survive in the 'post-apocalyptic' world after the catastrophe.
The 'catastrophe' portrayed typically represent the cultural fears of the time they were produced. Even some 'Christian' attempts to tell stories set in 'the end of days' have reinforced this limited & skewed understanding of the 'apocalypse'!

For readers of the book of Revelation, this is all unhelpful! The first word in the Greek text of Revelation is 'Ἀποκάλυψις', which:
  • when transliterated (from Greek letters to equivalent English letters) is 'apocalypsis'
         … which is the source of the English word 'apocalypse';
  • but when translated (to an English word with equivalent meaning), is an 'unveiling' or a 'revealing' of something hidden
         … that is, a revelation!

It is true that what is revealed by the author included a world-changing series of catastrophes. Given his heavy use of imagery & symbolism, however, it not immediately clear in what sense those visions should be understood or how the events they refer to may actually unfold in the earthly coontext we inhabit.

What is clear, however, is that the primary 'revelation' of the book is actually about the presence and activity of the Lord God and Jesus the Redeeming Messiah. This leads to the eventual overthrow of all evil, tyranny suffering & death. In its place, a new world order is to be established marked by the presence and peace of God throughout the world. Human civilisation will at last being made whole, thriving & fulfilling its best potentials & becoming a blessing at every level to all who are part of it.

This wonderful vision of God's plan for a world made new and truly good is almost completely missing from the modern 'apocalypses' of popular culture, but it is the heart and soul of the revelation the New Testament 'Apocalypse' gives us!
