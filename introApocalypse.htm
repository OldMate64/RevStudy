Apocalypse ... what Apocalypse?!
Why a 'Hitchhikers Guide'?
“Repent. The end is extremely f***ing nigh” - graffiti on a church wall in 28 Days Later
'Apocalypse' has meanings in conversational language & Western popular culture that are actually misleading for those seeking to read & understand the NT Apocalypse, the book of Revelation. Western literature & broadcast media over the last 2 centuries have increasingly given this ancient term dominating connotations of various 'end of the world as we know it' global (or at least national) catastrophes in many minds. Even 'Christian' attempts to tell stories set in the 'end of days' have often reinforced this limited & skewed understanding of 'apocalypse'!
For readers of the New Testament Apocalypse (the book of Revelation), this is all quite unhelpful!
It is true that what is revealed by the author does include a world-changing series of catastrophes (which of course is where the modern 'catastrophic' connotations of 'apocalypse' come from). Given the author's heavy use of culturally-related imagery & symbolism, however, scholars throughout the centuries have not found it immediately clear in exactly what sense those visions should be understood or how the events they refer to may actually unfold in our earthly coontext.
What is clear, however, is that the primary 'revelation' of the book is actually about the presence and activity of the Lord God and Jesus the Redeeming Messiah. This leads to the eventual overthrow of all evil, tyranny suffering & death. In its place, a new world order is to be established marked by the presence and peace of God throughout the world. Human civilisation will at last be made whole, thriving & fulfilling its best potentials to become a blessing at every level to all who are part of it.
This wonderful vision of God's plan for a world made new and truly good is almost completely missing from the modern 'apocalypses' of popular culture, but it is the heart and soul of the revelation the New Testament 'Apocalypse' gives us!
