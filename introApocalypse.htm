Apocalypse … what Apocalypse?!
Why a 'Hitchhikers Guide'?
“Repent … The end is extremely f***ing nigh” — church wall graffiti in 28 Days Later
'Apocalypse' has developed meanings in popular culture that are actually misleading for those seeking to read & understand the NT Apocalypse, the book of Revelation. This ancient term has developed dominant connotations of 'end of the world as we know it' global (or at least national) catastrophes in many minds. Journalism, for example, has increasingly used 'apocalypse' to refer to specific disasters & general secular cultural fears (eg. see The Conversation). Even 'Christian' attempts to tell stories set in 'the end of days' often reinforce this skewed understanding of 'apocalypse'!
For readers of the New Testament Apocalypse (the book of Revelation), this is all quite unhelpful!
It is true that the author's revelation does include visions of a world-changing series of symbolic catastrophes (which of course is where the modern 'catastrophic' connotations of 'apocalypse' come from). Given the author's heavy use of culturally-related imagery & symbolism, however, scholars throughout the centuries have not found it obvious in exactly what sense those visions & symbols should be understood or how the events they refer to may actually appear as they unfold in our earthly contexts.
What is clear, however, is the primary 'revelation' of the book … the presence, reign & activity of the Lord God & Jesus the Redeeming Messiah will lead to the eventual overthrow of all evil, tyranny, suffering & death. In its place, a new world order is to be established that is marked by the presence & shalom of God throughout the world. Human civilisation will at last be made whole & harmonious, thriving & fulfilling its best potentials to become a blessing at every level to all those in it & to all Creation.
This wonderful vision of God's plan for a world made new & truly good is almost completely missing from the modern 'apocalypses' of popular culture (& even those of Christian sub-cultures), but it is the heart & soul of the revelation the New Testament 'Apocalypse' gives us!
